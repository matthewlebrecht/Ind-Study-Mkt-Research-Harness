## 1. What the project is

Independent study, Matthew Lebrecht, sponsored by Talbot West. It builds **harnesses** —
small, versioned, auditable collectors — that gather evidence about how operationally
complex mid-size private companies modernize, and compares that against what the provider
market messages about.

Governing question: *what modernization pressures do operationally complex companies
articulate and act upon, where do buyer statements/actions and seller messaging converge or
diverge, and how adequately does the provider market address the gaps?*

Evidence lands in one Excel workbook (`data/market_intel_db.xlsx`) as **Observations**,
each tagged with an `evidence_role`:

| role | meaning | source example |
|---|---|---|
| `buyer_acts` | behaviour visible in records the company didn't write | OSHA inspections, carrier registry, job postings |
| `buyer_articulates` | what the company or its people say | press releases, executive interviews, employee reviews |
| `provider_market_responds` | what vendors message about | consultancy service pages |

The universe is fixed and given: **8 pilot companies** (`C0001`–`C0008`), **100 Anvil
companies** (`A001`–`A100`), **12 providers** (`P001`–`P012`, stored in `Companies` with
`qualification_status = provider_benchmark`).

## 4. THE TRAP — read this part twice

The project has one characteristic failure, recorded as **convention 16**: *a loose pattern
manufactures confidence; it does not merely add noise.* It had hit 3 times before this
session. **It hit six more times in this session alone.** Assume it will hit you.

What it produced this session:

| What was extracted | Where from | What it actually was |
|---|---|---|
| An executive named **"How We"**, titled President and CEO | Kenco About page | "How We Work" heading fragment |
| An executive named **"Become An"** | Rycon | "Become An Employee-Owner" banner |
| **"Corporate Governance Guidelines"** as a person | Merit Medical | A PDF link — *and it outranked the real roster page, so one bad name displaced 100% of the good ones* |
| A-grade evidence about **Prime Inc.** (trucking) | PR wires | Releases about Digital Prime Technologies, Prime Data Centers, Primech, Prime Electric |
| **163 of 248** first-party observations | Press releases | Single generic term: "workforce management" ×48, "integration" ×26, "cybersecurity" ×18, "ai" ×12 |
| A quote attributed to **Tom McGough** | A page about someone else | "McGough" appears nearby as the *company* name |

Three rules came out of it (now conventions **31–33** in `docs/conventions.md`):

**31. A single common token is never an identity.** Convention 11 was written about
`core/resolution.py`, but it applies anywhere a name meets text. The test must scale with
how distinctive the name is: coined tokens (MIDMARK, KENCO, RYCON) stand alone; dictionary
words (PRIME, SUMMIT, LIBERTY) need the full company phrase. See
`harnesses/h_firstparty_01/article.py::is_about_company`.

**32. An unsupported claim gets an admission threshold, not a strength downgrade.** Writing
a weak row at `weak_clue` and letting review sort it out is wrong when the evidence doesn't
support the claim *at all* — it still enters every count and still costs reviewer time.
Decide admission first, strength second.

**33. Audit a new harness's first output row by row before trusting its coverage number.**
Every defect above was **invisible in the run summary and obvious in the rows**.
`H-FIRSTPARTY-01` reported 97% coverage on a run where two thirds of its observations did
not support their own claim.

### The meta-lesson, which is the one I'd most want you to have

My *first* fix was wrong in the opposite direction **twice**:

- Tightening the company-identity check to "always require the full phrase" fixed Prime Inc.
  and then wrongly dropped Midmark, whose releases say "Midmark", not "Midmark Corporation."
- The OSHA pagination fix worked, then reported 40 inspections as an *exact* count — which
  would have replaced a **declared** cap at 20 with an **invisible** one at 40. Strictly
  worse: the first announced itself, the second would have looked like a measurement.

Both were caught only by re-checking after the fix. **Check your correction against the
case that was previously working, not just the case that was broken.**

## 5. What is NOT verified — do not overstate these

**`H-EMPREVIEW-01` has never produced a `covered` outcome from a live fetch.** Treat v1.0 as
built and unit-tested, not field-validated. No harness run has been written to the workbook
for it, deliberately — an `HR` row would assert it "ran."

The reason is an access finding, and it is the main result of that build. Both commissioned
sources are closed to compliant automated collection, measured against published robots.txt
for the `ClaudeBot` / `anthropic-ai` identities:

```
glassdoor.com    Disallow: /       (also HTTP 403 to any unauthenticated fetch)
indeed.com       Disallow: /cmp/   -- exactly where company reviews live
careerbliss.com  Disallow: /
```

Indeed is the instructive case: `/cmp/` is restricted for the crawler identities while the
narrower `Claude-User` / `Claude-SearchBot` groups are unrestricted there. An unattended
pass over 108 companies archiving every page is the crawler profile, not the user-initiated
one, so the stricter group governs. **Do not switch user-agent to get through this.** That
is evasion, not a fix, and the archive would rest on access the publisher declined to grant.
`core/robots.py` enforces it; a refusal becomes an `access_blocked` attempt with a citable
reason.

Comparably (the one permitted substitute with US coverage) serves real data — 12
`reviewBody` entries and an `EmployerAggregateRating` per company — then rate-limits to
~1–2 requests per cold window. kununu returns a JS challenge with no content.

Why this matters: **family 4 is the portfolio's only instrument for `legacy_constraint`.**
`H-FIRSTPARTY-01` structurally cannot produce one — a press release does not announce a
company's own obsolescence. A quiet zero here would license the conclusion that employees
aren't complaining about systems, when the truth is the harness isn't permitted to look.

## 6. Architecture you must not break

- **`core/db.py` is the only module that writes to the workbook** (convention 28). A harness
  that writes directly cannot be made to log its own misses.
- **Attempts live in a run context** (`core/attempts.py`). A harness *declares* its scope on
  open; close reconciles declared vs emitted and auto-writes `not_covered` for gaps. This is
  structural precisely so it doesn't break on the first early `continue`.
- **`core/topics.py` is the shared theme spine.** Both market sides classify into the same
  nine keys or the convergence analysis becomes string-matching. Buyer-side *patterns* live
  per-harness (see `h_execvoice_01/quotes.py::BUYER_VOICE_PATTERNS`) but must resolve into
  existing theme keys — there's a test enforcing that.
- **Natural key for Observations is `(company_id, harness_id, topic, source_url)`** and must
  be stable across runs (convention 30). Re-runs reconcile; they never overwrite human
  review.
- **`Attempts` is append-only and never reconciles** (convention 24).
- **Version bumps that invalidate prior rows set `reprocessing_required: true`**
  (convention 27), and you must `delete_observations(keep_reviewed=True)` before re-running —
  reconciliation alone leaves stale rows in place, because a corrected run doesn't propose
  them.
- New source or signal type → `scripts/register_sources.py`, then
  `scripts/sync_harness_sources.py --apply`, or `validate_repo_db.py` fails.

## 8. Operational notes (environment gotchas I hit)

- **Git had no author identity configured at any level** — `git commit` failed outright with
  `Author identity unknown`. This is why commits kept "getting blocked" in prior sessions.
  Now set repo-locally to the user's name/email.
- **Use `python -u`** for long runs. Buffered stdout means a redirected log stays empty for
  20 minutes and looks hung.
- **Never run two harnesses concurrently** — both call `db.save()` and will clobber each
  other. Chain them.
- **Don't cache non-200 responses from rate-limited sources.** A cached 403 replays a
  transient throttle as the source's permanent answer. `h_empreview_01/source.py` writes only
  on 200; the shared `DatedCache` caches everything, which is right for stable sources.
- Apostrophes inside `bash` heredocs break this environment's shell wrapper. Use the Write
  tool for source files.
- The workbook is ~3 MB; `openpyxl.load_workbook(read_only=True)` and iterate — a naive full
  read of `Observations` with per-row `.cell()` calls times out.

## 9. If you do one thing

Run a harness, then **read twenty of its rows before you believe its coverage number.**
That single habit found every defect worth finding this session, and the summary line found
none of them.
